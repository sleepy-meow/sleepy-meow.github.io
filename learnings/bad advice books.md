# rachel botsman - how to trust and be trusted
If you've read more in depth about any of the topics this short audiobook tries to cover in 3 hours, this might be an infuriating read.

The book 100% ignores the role of power dynamics, and manages to make a dozen complex topics sound like they are more simple than they actually are. It mixes up trust and psychological safety, leaves out relevant 

In one of her presentations she also argues that too much transparency reduces trust. But she leaves out an important piece of context that she does mention in her book, that the context was about micromanagement.

The overall vibe is that it ignores anything that would result in an uncomfortable conversation, and make it easier to sell consulting services. 

for a better take on trust:
> [!TIP] david desteno - the truth about trust

# 5 dysfunctions of a team
It locates the problem almost entirely in interpersonal behavior, and also ignores the role of structures: incentives, power asymmetry, workload, who actually holds decision rights.

It reads like an advertisement for the myers briggs personality test, which is widely regarded as pseudoscience. This might have not been as obvious when the book was written (the peak hype years of this test) but it doesn't age very well. 

the book is also based on anecdotal evidence rather than actual research.

In addition to that it takes an approach of forced vulnerability (add link), which reduces psychological safety. 

It does have a point that without trust nothing else works either, but just like maslow's hierarchy of needs, it oversimplifies more complex things into a simple pyramid. and just as maslow's 'research' doesn't survive any closer inspection, neither do the arguments in this book. 

The sequel read more like reluctant employees are just playing along to not get fired.

(add the very cringy quotes)

lastly, the name of the book. seeing teams as just a set of problems to be solved

Edmondson's psychological safety work is useful contrast, since she treats safety as a property of the team's climate that leaders and structure largely produce, rather than a disposition individuals need to muster. Lencioni asks people to be braver; Edmondson asks what makes speaking up costly in the first place.
> [!TIP] amy edmondson - the fearless organization

for a better take on vulnerability:
> [!TIP] anything by brene brown

> "we can't demand people to trust us, or instruct them to come up with big ideas. and certainly can't demand people to cooperate. these are always results of feeling safe and trusted"
