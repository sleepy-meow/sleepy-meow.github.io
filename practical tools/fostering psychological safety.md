# simple things to say
a few simple, uncommon, powerful phrases that anyone can utter to make the workplace feel just a tiny bit more psychologically safe:
*I don't know.*
*I need help.*
*I made a mistake.*
*I'm sorry.*

Each of these is an expression of vulnerability. By being willing to acknowledge that you are a fallible human being, you give permission to others to do likewise.

Similarly powerful in shaping the climate even if you are not the boss are words of interest and availability. For example, most of us face many opportunities to say things like these:
*What can I do to help?*
*What are you up against?*
*What are your concerns?*
# The leader's self-check questions

Setting the Stage
- Have I spoken of failures in the right way for the nature of the work? Do I point out that small failures are the currency of improvement — that you can't get something brand new "right the first time"?
- Have I articulated clearly *why our work matters, and for whom*?
- Even if it seems obvious for my industry, how often do I talk about *what's at stake*?

Inviting Participation
- Situational humility: Have I made sure people know *I don't think I have all the answers*? Have I emphasized we can always learn more, and that this situation requires everyone to be humble and curious?
- Proactive inquiry: How often do I ask *good questions rather than rhetorical ones*? How often do I ask questions of others rather than just expressing my view? Do I mix questions that go broad and deep?
- Systems & structures: Have I created structures to *systematically elicit* ideas and concerns? Are they well designed to ensure a safe environment for open dialogue?

Responding Productively
- Express appreciation: Have I *listened thoughtfully*, signaling that what I'm hearing matters? Do I *acknowledge or thank* the speaker for bringing the idea or question?
- Destigmatize failure: Have I done what I can to *destigmatize failure*? What more can I do to *celebrate intelligent failures*? When someone brings bad news, how do I make it a *positive experience*? Do I *offer help* to guide next steps?
- Sanction clear violations: Have I *clarified the boundaries* — do people know what counts as blameworthy? Do I respond to clear violations in an appropriately tough way to influence future behavior?
