# analogy to leadership at the workplace
It's easy to adapt a mindset of responsibility when the consequences are immediate danger of drowning. It's easier to ignore when sitting comfortably in an office, especially when you don't see the impact your decisions might have on others. 



And at this point I was slowly becoming aware of my own incompetence. 

Having some experience in sailing over 15 years ago, I recognized the urge to appear like I had a better idea what I was doing than I did. I hesitated asking questions that I wasn't sure anymore whether they were obvious or not, but quickly realized that the sea doesn't give a shit. It doesn't care if you were uncomfortable to ask silly questions. 


Sailing is a good example of where hierarchy makes sense, for practical reasons. But it has less to do with being in control, but about responsibility. That ultimately, you are responsible for making sure everyone on board makes it safely back home. 

The thing that crossed my mind wasn't 'bwahaha finally i can be the one to tell others what to do', but to start learning about what safety equipment to buy, what skills to learn to not end up in a situation I wouldn't be able to get out of, either because of the boat, the skills of others on board, weather conditions, or my own incompetence to handle those conditions.

# taking calculated risks

There's probably nothing more humbling than getting caught up in waves that are a bit too big than you're comfortable with, realizing that all your life decisions up until this point will decide your fate. Where all you can do is hold on to something, and think to yourself that the ship is designed to handle the beating it's currently taking, and that you've prepared in case it won't.
