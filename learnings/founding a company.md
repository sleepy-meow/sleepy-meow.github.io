# difficult conversations
spicy topics aren't comfortable, but they're a whole lot less uncomfortable to have beforehand than putting the horse blinkers on and hoping it won't be a problem later on.

# explicit agreements
Writing down simple agreements about ownership, responsibilities, IP, what if someone wants to leave, (or what if others want someone to leave) and other things before it's actually needed may be 100x easier than figuring these things out when problems emerge.

> [!TIP] Eric Ries - Incorruptible

# being on the same page
just like any relationship, there's a long list of things that are more comfortable to ignore or think they won't matter

 some examples:
- why do you want to found a company
- ambition levels
- personal runway if things get tough (how urgently does each founder need to go look for a new job)
- initial acceptable salary
- what if someone get sick / needs to drop out for other reasons
- project scope
- opinions about hiring people
- preferred ways of working
- culture / general world view
- ownership / investments
- how to handle disagreements / decision making

# preferred leadership style
When a team is small, it's easy to overlook this and just focus on the product, thinking that leadership skills are not needed. But there's a difference between *"i don't want a boss to tell me what to do"*, and *"i want to be the one to tell others what to do"*. 

# working together beforehand
Ideally spending some time working together first without too many commitments, to see how the team works together & get to know each other.

Knowing each persons strengths and interests also can help figure out whether all the ingredients are there. (also there will always be things to do that aren't fun)

Find a way to see how the team works together on a bad day, when everyone is tired, when things don't go as planned (maybe a vacation where everything goes wrong :D)
